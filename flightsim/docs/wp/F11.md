# F11: Nose-wheel steering for taxiing

Phase 1.4 · Effort: high · Depends: none (board: after F5, F8 and F9, which share
`assets/aircraft/*.ini`) · Branch: `wp/F11-nosewheel-steering`

## Why

The owner (2026-11-09): "Simulated nose wheel steering on ground. Under 35 kt we should be able to
use rudder input to turn the aircraft precisely on the ground, otherwise taxiways are useless since
low speed has no rudder authority."

Today the right stick X drives `ControlInputs::steeringNorm` (= rudder), and `FlightModel` writes
it to `fcs/steer-cmd-norm` scaled by a gain that fades from 1 at `[ground] steer_full_below_kt`
(15) to `steer_min_frac` at `steer_min_above_kt` (`FlightModel.cpp`, the end of the input block).
JSBSim turns that command into `cmd × max_steer` of each steerable gear, and the stock models'
`max_steer` is small: c172p, c182 and c310 10°, pa28 15°, global5000, B747 and A320 **5°** (737
35°, MD11 60°, 787-8 70°). At 5° an A320 (wheelbase about 12.6 m) turns on a 145 m radius and a
B747 on about 290 m: no taxiway turn can be flown. Real airliners steer with a tiller (±70–78°)
below taxi speed and the pedals (±7°) above; light aircraft add differential braking.

The planner found that JSBSim 1.3.3 ties a writable `fcs/steer-pos-deg[i]` to every steerable,
non-castered gear (`FGLGear::bind`, `SetSteerAngleDeg`, no clamp), so the game can set the nose
wheel angle in degrees without an overlay copy of each model.

## Do

1. **Measure first** (`steeringTest.cpp`, print-only lines `STEER <id> <what> <value>`): for all
   ten aircraft on the XTST runway, parking brake off, idle, a speed-hold on the throttle and brakes
   at 8, 15, 25 and 35 kt: the steady turn radius (m) and yaw rate (°/s) with 25 %, 50 % and full
   right stick; and the nose-wheel angle reached. Keep the before numbers for the PR.
2. **Tiller by lateral acceleration.** New `[ground]` keys: `tiller_max_deg` (the real tiller or
   steering limit: airliners 70–75, global5000 about 60, light aircraft 30, standing in for pedal
   steering plus differential braking), `wheelbase_ft` (nose gear to main gear, from the model's
   contact points), `steer_lat_g` (default 0.3). On the ground below `tiller_below_kt` (default
   35), the nose-wheel angle is `input × min(tiller_max_deg, atan(wheelbase × steer_lat_g × g /
   v²))` — full lock at walking pace, a natural fade with speed so the aircraft cannot skid or tip
   (about 54° at 10 kt, 19° at 20 kt and 6° at 35 kt for an A320). Write it in degrees to every
   `fcs/steer-pos-deg[i]` that exists, after the existing `fcs/steer-cmd-norm` write, each step.
   Above `tiller_below_kt` (or when `tiller_max_deg` is 0), today's pedal steering with its fade
   is unchanged, so the takeoff and landing rolls do not change.
3. **Precision:** a steering curve on the ground, `x · (1 − e + e · x²)` with `[ground]
   steer_expo` (default 0.5), so small stick moves give small corrections and full stick still
   gives full lock. The rudder surface keeps the unshaped input.
4. **Rate limit:** the nose-wheel angle follows the command at most `steer_rate_deg_s` (default
   40 °/s, airliners 20), like a hydraulic tiller, so a stick flick does not snap the nose.
5. Values per aircraft from the model geometry and the measurement; one comment line each naming
   the source (`wheelbase from the model's NOSE and MAIN contact x`).
